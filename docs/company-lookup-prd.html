01

Feature Overview

Company Lookup is a fourth lead source inside the existing Add Leads dialog, alongside Apollo Search, Excel / CSV and Manual Entry.

Where the existing Apollo Search finds people by industry segment, Company Lookup finds them at one named company. A manager enters a company name, selects the correct organization from Apollo's matches, reviews the people who work there at no credit cost, selects up to five, and only those five enter the paid enrichment pipeline.

Company name → find companies → select company → find people → select up to 5 → reveal emails → existing lead pipeline

The feature adds no new enrichment logic. Selected contacts join the existing lead, reveal, scraping, drafting, assignment and campaign systems at the same point the current Apollo Search joins them.

02

Problem / Client Use Case

The client regularly hears — through trade contacts, exhibitions or industry word of mouth — that a particular company exports or manufactures plastic in volume. For example, "ABC Plastics".

They know the name. They typically do not know the domain, the exact legal entity, or who to contact there. The platform currently offers no way to act on this: Apollo Search filters by industry, title and location only, so reaching one specific named company means searching an entire segment and paying to reveal everyone in it.

A company name alone creates two distinct problems:

  • Ambiguity. Many companies share a name. "ABC Plastics Ltd" in Kenya is a different business from "ABC Plastic Group" in Nigeria. Only the client can determine which one was meant.
  • Cost. Once the correct company is identified, revealing every employee wastes credits on people who will never be contacted.

Company Lookup addresses both: an explicit disambiguation step controlled by the user, followed by a selection step that bounds the spend.

03

Goals

  • Enable a manager to go from a company name to usable, emailable contacts without knowing the domain.
  • Make company disambiguation explicit and user-driven; the system must never guess which company was intended.
  • Keep credit spend proportionate and predictable — one credit to search, one per contact revealed.
  • Display only contacts that can actually be emailed (has_email = true).
  • Surface the most commercially relevant people first through job-title ranking.
  • Route selected contacts into the existing lead and enrichment systems without creating a parallel pipeline.
  • Guarantee that candidates the user did not select can never trigger Apollo spend.
04

Non-Goals

  • Mobile / WhatsApp enrichment. Out of scope for V1. No webhook infrastructure will be designed or built. Raised as Business Question 1.
  • Unrestricted pagination. V1 supports bounded, explicitly requested paging up to three Apollo pages — see §6 Step 2. Walking Apollo's full 500-page limit is not supported, because each page consumes a credit.
  • Exposing the full Apollo filter set. Advanced Search offers a small, curated group of organization filters rather than every supported parameter.
  • Adding contacts at organizations already held. Organizations already in the system are blocked from proceeding — see §13.2. Expanding coverage at a known company is a separate workflow.
  • Bulk lookup. One company per lookup. No multi-company paste workflow.
  • A new lead_source value. V1 uses the existing apollo source — see §9 for why this is load-bearing.
  • Rebuilding downstream systems. Enrichment, reveal, duplicate handling, scraping, AI drafting, campaigns, assignment, imports and watchdogs are reused without modification.
  • New database tables. None are required — see §10.
  • Automatic contact selection. The system never pre-selects contacts on the user's behalf.

Pagination and refinement

Apollo charges one credit per page of up to 100 results, and returns a pagination object carrying page, per_page, total_entries and total_pages. Confirmed — Apollo docs Two consequences shape the design:

  • Request per_page = 100. The credit is charged per page regardless of how many results are returned, so requesting fewer wastes the credit already spent. One credit should buy the maximum available.
  • Page through those results in the browser at no cost. Displaying 100 rows at once is unusable, so the interface pages through the already-retrieved set — roughly 20 at a time — with free Previous / Next controls. This is the paging users will actually need, and it costs nothing.

Fetching Apollo page 2 is a separate, explicit action labelled with its cost — for example Search 100 more companies (1 credit) — placed only at the end of the already-retrieved results, and requiring confirmation. It is never triggered by scrolling, by the free in-page controls, or automatically.

V1 caps this at three Apollo pages, or 300 companies, per search session. Beyond that the interface stops offering more pages and directs the user to refine instead. The reasoning: if the intended company is not among 300 relevance-ordered candidates, further paging is a poor use of credits compared with adding a country, a website, or an Advanced filter. The cap is a product decision, not an Apollo limit — Apollo itself permits 500 pages. Proposed

The header of the results area always states the position plainly, for example: "Showing 1–20 of 100 retrieved · 342 companies match in Apollo · 1 credit spent". This tells the user how much exists, how much has been paid for, and what another page would cost, before any further spend.

Single-select only. Re-running the search with changed inputs is a new search and consumes another credit, so that action carries an explicit cost warning.

Select People

Proposed
  • Only people with an available email are displayed. This is filtered at the Apollo request via contact_email_status, so Apollo returns only contactable people rather than the platform fetching a full roster and discarding half of it. The existing searchPeople() already sends this parameter. Confirmed — implementation The returned has_email flag is still checked on our side as a safety net.
  • Apollo does not return the address itself at this stage, only an indicator that one exists. Confirmed — Apollo docs
  • Surnames are partially masked at this stage. People Search returns last_name_obfuscated (for example Do***e) rather than the full surname, so the selection list displays first name, masked surname and job title. The full name arrives only after the paid reveal. Confirmed — Apollo docs
  • Results are ranked by job title, with senior and commercially relevant roles first — CEO, Founder, Managing Director, Director, Head, Export Manager, Procurement Manager, then all others.
  • Seniority is not returned by People Search, which is why ranking is derived from the job title rather than a seniority field. Confirmed — Apollo docs
  • Multi-select with a hard ceiling of 5. A persistent counter reads n / 5 selected and remaining checkboxes disable once the limit is reached.
  • Contacts already present as leads, or already recorded as unenrichable, are shown but not selectable.
  • Nothing on this screen is written to the database.

Key research outcome — endpoint alignment

Apollo's People Search documentation specifies the endpoint path /api/v1/mixed_people/api_search, which is exactly the path this platform already calls, and that documentation lists organization_ids[] as a supported parameter. The organization-scoped people search this feature depends on is therefore documented on the same endpoint already in production use, and no new Apollo integration pattern is introduced.

Organization Search — request and response

Confirmed request parameters relevant to this feature: q_organization_name, organization_locations[], organization_not_locations[], q_organization_domains_list[], organization_num_employees_ranges[], organization_ids[], q_organization_keyword_tags[], revenue_range[min|max], page, per_page. Confirmed — Apollo docs

The response returns an organizations array. Each organization carries an id — Apollo's organization identifier — together with primary_domain, website_url, linkedin_url, logo_url, founded_year and phone. Confirmed — Apollo docs

Ambiguity to resolve. The published field listing does not clearly enumerate employee count, country, city, state or industry on this response, while secondary API references describe employee count and location as present. This documentation is ambiguous and the exact field names must be confirmed against a live response before the results table in §6 Step 2 is finalised. Requires verification

Authentication: a Master API key is described as acceptable but not required for this endpoint. Confirmed — Apollo docs

People Search — response fields

Per person, the response provides id (Apollo person identifier), first_name, last_name_obfuscated, title, availability indicators including has_email, and an organization object describing the employer. Confirmed — Apollo docs

The response explicitly does not include the full surname, the email address, phone numbers, or a seniority field. Contact details require the paid enrichment endpoint. Confirmed — Apollo docs

Whether the nested organization object on People Search returns actual employee counts or only availability booleans is unclear from the documentation and is not required by this feature, since organization attributes are taken from the Organization Search response instead.

Pagination behaviour and limits

Organization Search permits a maximum of 100 records per page and 500 pages, for a documented display limit of 50,000 records. Confirmed — Apollo docs

The response includes a pagination object containing page, per_page, total_entries and total_pages. Confirmed — Apollo docs This is what allows the interface to tell the user how many companies match in total, and therefore whether refining or paging is the better next step.

Credits are charged per page requested, not per result returned — a page yielding three companies costs the same as one yielding a hundred. Confirmed — Apollo docs V1 therefore always requests the maximum page size and treats each additional Apollo page as a separate, explicitly priced action, capped at three pages per search session. The behavioural detail is specified in §6 Step 2.

Credit / Cost Behaviour

Representative lookup in which the user selects three contacts:

StageDetailCredits
Organization Searchpage 1, results returned1
People Searchany number of people at that organization0

At the V1 maximum of five contacts and a single Apollo page, the intended spend is 1 + 5 = 6 credits. If the user requests the maximum of three Apollo pages and selects five contacts, the intended spend is 3 + 5 = 8 credits.

Credit visibility — cost is stated before every charge

No action in this feature consumes a credit without the cost being displayed on the control that triggers it. There are exactly three credit-consuming moments, and each is surfaced:

ActionCostHow it is surfaced
Search Companies1Stated on the search button and beside the form before the first search.
Request another Apollo page1Explicit labelled action — "Search 100 more companies (1 credit)" — requiring confirmation. Never automatic. Capped at three pages.
Re-run a changed search1Treated as a new search; the cost warning is repeated when inputs change.
Import selected contacts1 eachThe running counter shows the reveal cost as contacts are selected; the confirmation restates it, e.g. "Import 3 contacts — 3 credits".

Free actions are also labelled as free, so users are not deterred from using them: paging within already-retrieved results, listing people at a selected organization, and refining a search that returned no companies all carry no charge.

The results header additionally reports credits already spent in the current session alongside the number of companies retrieved and the total matching in Apollo, so the decision to page again or refine is always an informed one.

Intended spend is not a guaranteed ceiling

Apollo bills a bulk_match request on receipt, including requests that fail or return no email. The existing pipeline therefore permits a second and final attempt per person — MAX_ENRICH_ATTEMPTS = 2, enforced at the point of candidate selection so the limit holds even if downstream cleanup fails. Confirmed — implementation

Absolute worst case for a single lookup: 1 search credit plus 2 credits per selected contact — 11 credits if all five contacts require their retry. In normal operation a reveal succeeds on the first attempt and the retry is never used.

Three existing protections bound this further. A wholesale Apollo refusal such as an exhausted balance or a rate limit releases leads unpenalised and reverts their attempt counters, so an outage never consumes a lifetime attempt. The candidate list is trimmed to the real remaining balance before anything is claimed. A person Apollo cannot resolve is archived permanently rather than re-queried. Confirmed — implementation

No-result searches are free

Apollo documents that a search returning no results consumes 0 credits, and this behaviour is described consistently across its search endpoints. A company name that matches nothing therefore costs nothing. Confirmed — Apollo docs

Credit ledger accuracy

The Organization Search credit must be written to the existing credit ledger so that Settings → Keys → Usage remains accurate. That screen totals credits by reading the logged payload across Apollo-sourced log rows without filtering on event name, so exactly one ledger row per search keeps the figure correct. A duplicate row double-counts; a missing row silently under-reports. Confirmed — implementation

Open technical risk. Apollo operates several credit categories. Secondary documentation indicates that API search endpoints draw on export credits and that API usage shares the same monthly pool as the platform interface, but this is not stated unambiguously in the primary endpoint documentation. The platform's balance check reads one specific counter; if Organization Search decrements a different one, the pre-flight balance check will not account for this spend and the usage screen will under-report it. This is resolvable definitively by reading the balance, issuing one Organization Search request, and reading the balance again. Requires verification

09

Lead & Enrichment Flow

Selected contacts join the existing pipeline at precisely the point the current Apollo Search joins it. No second enrichment path is created.

Company Lookup → selected contact
→ create imports row + reuse or create organizations row
→ insert leads row: lead_source = 'apollo', has_email = true, email = null
→ existing enrich route claims the lead
→ Apollo people/bulk_match (1 credit)
→ email written to leads.email; status recomputed by database trigger
→ organization scraping · AI drafting · assignment · campaigns

Implementation constraint — lead_source must remain 'apollo'

Both the enrichment route and the daily rescue watchdog filter candidates on lead_source = 'apollo'. A lead labelled apollo_company would be selected by neither, and would remain in the has_email = true, email = null state permanently — never revealed and never rescued. Confirmed — implementation

Using apollo therefore requires no changes to the enrichment pipeline. Introducing a separate label would require editing both filters and the source enum used by the leads list. This underpins the V1 decision; the trade-off is restated as Business Question 2.

Implementation constraint — has_email must be set explicitly

leads.has_email is nullable with no database default. An insert that omits the field stores NULL, and every enrichment selector matches on has_email = true. The contact would appear imported but would never be enriched, with no error raised anywhere. The insert must set this field explicitly. Confirmed — implementation

10

Database Impact

No migration required

Every field this feature stores already exists. The organizations table carries the Apollo organization ID, domain, website, industry, keywords, employee count, city and country. leads and imports require no new columns. lead_source and imports.source are free-text with no CHECK constraint, so labelling requires no DDL. The credit ledger totals spend by reading log payloads, so the new search credit appears in Settings usage without schema work. Confirmed — implementation

No temporary storage table for candidate people. None is technically necessary: every enrichment selector reads exclusively from leads, so unselected candidates can be held in browser state and discarded. Persisting them would create the exact risk §14 exists to prevent.

Existing uniqueness constraints that must be respected

TableConstraintConsequence for this feature
organizationsunique (company_id, apollo_org_id)Company Lookup is the first flow to know the Apollo organization ID at insert time; it must merge rather than insert.
organizationsunique (company_id, domain) where domain is not nullA second collision path: an organization already stored under a different name but the same domain.
leadsunique (company_id, apollo_id)Prevents re-inserting a contact that has already been imported.
leadsunique (company_id, lower(email)) where activeTwo Apollo person records sharing one mailbox. Handled by existing logic added 8 August 2026.

All four constraints are scoped per company_id, which makes tenant-scoped database access mandatory — see §18.

Deferred by design. A cache of company searches would prevent a repeated identical search from consuming a second credit. At the expected volume this does not justify a table and its expiry rules; holding results in browser state already covers navigating back and forth within a single session. This should be revisited if the usage log shows repeat searches occurring in practice.

Duplicate / Existing Organization Behaviour

13.1 Determining whether an Apollo company already exists

Apollo assigns every organization a stable identifier, returned as id on the Organization Search response. Confirmed — Apollo docs The platform stores this value as organizations.apollo_org_id, protected by a unique index on (company_id, apollo_org_id). Confirmed — implementation

Matching must proceed in a defined order, because the three available identifiers differ sharply in reliability:

OrderMatch onReliabilityAction on match
1 apollo_org_id Highest. Apollo's own identifier, stable across searches and unique per company in our schema. Definitive match. Reuse the existing organization row.
2 Normalised domain High. Backed by a unique index, but nullable and occasionally changes. Treat as the same organization. Reuse the row and populate the missing apollo_org_id.
3 Company name Low. Name collision is the entire premise of this feature. Never merge automatically. Surface as a possible duplicate for the user to judge.

Why the order matters — organizations with a NULL Apollo organization ID

Organizations created by the existing keyword search are inserted with apollo_org_id = NULL and no domain; those fields are populated later, during the paid reveal. Confirmed — implementation A substantial number of existing organization records will therefore not match on step 1 even though they represent the same company.

Step 2 exists precisely to catch these. When a domain match is found on a record whose apollo_org_id is NULL, the correct action is to enrich the existing record with the Apollo organization ID now known — not to create a second row. This progressively repairs historical records as the feature is used.

Step 3 must never trigger an automatic merge. The existing keyword-import flow matches organizations by name, which is a known source of near-duplicate organization records. Extending name matching into an automatic merge would compound that problem rather than contain it.

Tenant scoping. All uniqueness constraints are scoped per company_id. Every existence check must run through the tenant-scoped database client so that one tenant's organizations are never matched against another's. The scoped client applies this filter automatically; the unscoped administrative client does not. Confirmed — implementation

13.2 Existing organizations — blocked from proceeding

Product decision for V1 — settled

If the organization already exists in the system, Company Lookup does not allow the user to proceed to People Search for that organization.

Company Lookup exists to discover a company we do not already have and to add contacts from it. Where the organization is already present and already has leads, finding additional people at that same organization is not part of this workflow.

Required behaviour when a search result matches an existing organization:

  • Display the company, labelled Already in system.
  • Do not allow it to be selected; the row is disabled with a brief explanation.
  • Do not call People Search for that organization through this workflow.
  • Do not create another organization record.
  • Do not create duplicate leads.

This decision simplifies the flow: the organization matching described in §13.1 is used to exclude results rather than to merge into them, and no lookup-initiated write to an existing organization occurs. The merge-not-insert rule in §13.1 still governs the organization created for a genuinely new company, and remains necessary because a company absent from the organizations table can still collide on domain at insert time.

Users who need additional contacts at a company already held are directed to the existing organization record, where the current lead-management tools apply. Adding a dedicated route for expanding coverage at a known company may be considered for a later version; it is explicitly outside V1.

13.3 Duplicate contacts

Duplicate detection for contacts is performed on the Apollo person ID, scoped to the tenant — not on email address, and not on name. Confirmed — implementation This is appropriate here because the email address is unknown at selection time and the surname is partially masked, leaving the Apollo person ID as the only reliable identifier available before payment.

Two existing exclusion checks are reused:

  • Contacts already present in leads are surfaced as already imported and cannot be selected.
  • Contacts recorded in unenrichable_leads — previously paid for and confirmed to have no usable email — are excluded so they are never re-imported and re-charged.

Because organizations already held are blocked at step 2 (§13.2), contacts reaching the selection screen belong to an organization new to the system. These exclusion checks nonetheless remain necessary: the same person can exist as a lead under a different organization record, or can have been archived as unenrichable from an earlier import, and either case must not be paid for a second time.

14

Safety / Credit Protection

The controlling rule

In this platform, a lead row carrying has_email = true and email = null is the instruction to spend a credit. Background jobs claim any such row and reveal it automatically. Confirmed — implementation

If People Search returned 20 candidates and all 20 were written to leads purely to display them, the background job would reveal all 20 — spending up to 20 credits on contacts the user never selected.

Only user-selected contacts are ever inserted. Unselected candidates are held in browser state and discarded. Because every enrichment selector reads exclusively from leads, a contact that is never inserted is structurally invisible to the enrichment system. This is a guarantee by omission, which is stronger than any flag or filter.

Layered protections, all pre-existing:

  • Selection cap — maximum five contacts, enforced in the server-side validator, not only in the interface.
  • Attempt ceiling — two lifetime attempts per person, enforced when candidates are selected.
  • Balance pre-flight — checked before the Organization Search and again before any reveal; the candidate list is trimmed to what the balance can actually fund.
  • Development-workspace block — the internal workspace cannot spend from the shared client pool.
  • Ledger row — the Organization Search credit is logged so the usage screen remains accurate.
  • Unenrichable exclusion — contacts already proven to have no email are never offered for selection.

Acceptance Criteria

Criteria marked Requires verification depend on a live Apollo response and should be finalised once that verification is complete.

  1. Company Lookup appears as a fourth source in Add Leads, visible to managers only, and blocked in the internal/development workspace.
  2. A company-name search issues one Organization Search request with per_page = 100 and writes exactly one credit-ledger row, which appears in Settings → Keys → Usage.
  3. The ledger row reflects the same credit counter used by the pre-flight balance check. Requires verification
  4. A search returning no companies consumes no credits and displays an empty state that names what can be changed — spelling, country, website or Advanced filters.
  5. Country and Website are optional; a search using only a company name succeeds.
  6. Advanced Search is collapsed by default, opens empty, and exposes every remaining Organization Search parameter grouped as company profile, location, funding and hiring signals. The basic form remains three fields.
  7. Active Advanced filters remain visible as removable chips when the panel is collapsed, so results are never narrowed by a filter the user cannot see.
  8. The people request sends contact_email_status so Apollo returns only contactable people; has_email is additionally checked on the response.
  9. Applying Advanced filters changes the results returned and does not change the per-page credit cost.
  10. Results display enough information to distinguish similarly named companies — name, location, employees, website and system status. Exact fields displayed depend on the confirmed response shape. Requires verification
  11. Selection is always explicit. A result set of exactly one company is not auto-selected and does not auto-advance; the first result is never assumed correct; no relevance-based auto-selection occurs in any result state.
  12. Paging within already-retrieved results consumes no credits and is available without confirmation.
  13. Requesting a further Apollo page is an explicit, labelled, confirmed action stating its one-credit cost, is never triggered by scrolling or by in-page controls, and is capped at three Apollo pages per search session.
  14. The results header reports companies retrieved, total matching in Apollo from the pagination object, and credits spent in the session.
  15. Every credit-consuming control states its cost before activation; free actions are labelled as free.
  16. Organizations already present in the system are labelled Already in system, rendered non-selectable, and cannot be advanced past step 2.
  17. No People Search request is issued for an organization already present in the system.
  18. Blocked organizations produce no new organization row, no organization update and no leads.
  19. An organization matched only by company name is not treated as already in the system and does not block selection.
  20. Selecting a new company lists its people using organization_ids[] and consumes zero credits.
  21. Only people with has_email = true are shown, ranked with senior and commercial titles first, displaying first name, masked surname and job title.
  22. A maximum of five people can be selected. The counter reads n / 5 selected, and the limit is enforced server-side as well as in the interface.
  23. On confirmation, only the selected people exist in leads. A database check immediately after import returns no rows for unselected candidates.
  24. Inserted leads carry lead_source = 'apollo', has_email = true, email = null, and valid company_id, organization_id and import_id values.
  25. The existing enrichment pipeline reveals those leads with no code change to the enrich route, the watchdog or the claim function.
  26. Total credits for a single-page, three-contact lookup equal four in the ledger — one search plus three reveals — with no unexplained additional spend.
  27. Organization matching follows the order defined in §13.1 — Apollo organization ID, then domain, then name as a non-binding signal only — and name-only matches never trigger an automatic merge.
  28. The organization created for a new company merges rather than blind-inserts, so neither the Apollo-organization-ID nor the domain uniqueness constraint can be violated.
  29. All organization and lead lookups are tenant-scoped; a company held by another tenant is never matched or reused.
  30. People already imported, or already recorded as unenrichable, cannot be selected.
  31. Revealed leads flow into scraping, drafting, assignment and campaigns exactly as leads from the existing Apollo Search do.
  32. No new database table or migration is required to ship V1.

Open Business Questions

The following items require business or product judgement and are deliberately unresolved in this document. Neither blocks V1 implementation.

Previously open, now settled

The question of whether Company Lookup should allow users to add new contacts at a company already held has been decided: it does not. Existing organizations are labelled and blocked from proceeding. The behaviour is specified in §13.2 and is no longer an open question.

Business Question 1 — Mobile / Phone Enrichment

Should mobile or phone enrichment be supported in a future version of Company Lookup?

Out of scope for V1 and not being built. Relevant context for the decision: Apollo charges substantially more for a returned mobile number than for an email address, and delivers phone data asynchronously to a webhook rather than in the API response.

Implications. Supporting mobile numbers is a meaningful cost and infrastructure commitment rather than a configuration toggle. It would require new webhook infrastructure, asynchronous result handling, and a substantially revised credit model. Apollo also does not indicate whether a mobile number is reachable on WhatsApp specifically, so any WhatsApp-related expectation should be confirmed against what Apollo actually provides before commitments are made. No V1 work depends on this decision.

Business Question 2 — Source and Reporting Label

Do Company Lookup leads require a separate source or reporting label, or is the existing apollo source sufficient?

V1 uses apollo, which requires no changes to the enrichment pipeline. A separate label would make these leads independently filterable and reportable.

Implications. Introducing a distinct source value requires editing the enrichment route filter, the daily watchdog filter and the leads-list source enum, as set out in §9. Omitting any of those changes would leave the affected leads permanently un-enriched. These leads also remain distinguishable today through their own import batch, so part of the reporting requirement may already be satisfied without a new source value. The decision should be based on whether batch-level reporting is sufficient for the business.

Implementation Notes / Technical Considerations

Non-negotiable implementation constraints

  • Use the tenant-scoped database client for all reads and writes. It automatically applies the company_id filter, stamps company_id on insert — a NOT NULL column on leads — and rewrites upsert conflict targets to include it. The unscoped administrative client does none of these.
  • Set has_email = true explicitly. The column is nullable with no default; omitting it produces a lead that is never enriched (§9).
  • Merge organizations following the order in §13.1. Two unique indexes can collide, and name matching must never merge automatically.
  • Never persist unselected candidates in any table the enrichment system reads (§14).
  • Write exactly one ledger row per Organization Search (§8).
  • Enforce the five-contact limit server-side, not only in the interface.

Points to settle during implementation

  • Job-title ranking rules. The ordering principle is agreed; the exact keyword list and matching strategy — exact, prefix or contains — should be defined in lib/constants.ts and kept editable without a deployment where practical. Title is confirmed as returned by People Search; seniority is not, so ranking must be derived from the title string alone.
  • Full company roster. The existing searchPeople() applies fixed title, seniority and employee-range filters suited to segment searching. A named-company lookup requires the full roster, so those filters need a bypass. This is a deliberate behavioural divergence from the existing search and should not be applied to it.
  • Masked surnames in the interface. Because People Search returns only an obfuscated surname, the selection list cannot display full names. Interface copy should make clear that the complete name is revealed after import rather than appearing to display incomplete data.
  • Handing off to enrichment. Both import_id and lead_ids are already accepted by the enrich route. import_id matches the existing pattern and inherits self-chaining and daily rescue behaviour; lead_ids is more explicit for a set of five. Either is acceptable.
  • Applying the optional website input. q_organization_domains_list[] is confirmed as a supported Organization Search parameter. Whether supplying it should narrow the search or merely re-rank results is a product refinement to settle during implementation.
  • Search-session state. Retrieved companies, the page count reached and the credits spent must persist while the user pages, refines within the wizard, or steps backwards, so that returning to the results does not silently re-purchase a page already paid for. Session state is held client-side; nothing is persisted to the database.
  • Existence checks run once per retrieved page. Each newly returned page is checked against the organizations table in a single batched query rather than per row, using the §13.1 matching order. Results are cached for the session so that free in-browser paging performs no further database work.
  • Advanced filter defaults. Advanced Search opens empty and applies nothing unless the user sets a value, so an unopened panel can never silently narrow a search. Active filters remain visible once the panel is collapsed, so a user cannot forget why results are limited.
  • Three-page cap placement. The cap is a product decision and should be a named constant alongside the contact limit, not a literal buried in the interface, so it can be adjusted once real usage shows whether three pages is the right ceiling.

Recommended pre-implementation verification

A single Organization Search request against a known company, paired with a balance reading immediately before and after, resolves the majority of outstanding technical unknowns: the exact response field names for employee count and location, and which credit counter the search decrements. A companion People Search request scoped by organization_ids[] confirms roster behaviour at no credit cost.

The Organization Search request costs one credit. Spending it is preferable to designing the results table and the credit accounting against an assumed response shape.
