Has the follow-up gone out?

The Sequences tab shows what we configured. Nothing shows what actually ran. Here is the view that answers it — plus two things the live data revealed that change the spec.

Written 18 Aug 2026 Covers meeting points 3, 4, 5 Numbers live, from the production database Status design only — nothing coded yet

§1What we can already answer

Good news first: no new tracking is needed. Every follow-up send is already recorded — Instantly confirms each one and we store the step number against the lead. The gap is purely that nothing reads it back.

That means all five requests are a read problem, not a plumbing problem. The numbers below are real, queried live while writing this.

§2Two findings that change the spec

Both surfaced while checking the client's example against real data. The second one is time-sensitive.

Finding A · affects the table design

“Sent / pending” is three buckets, not two

The client sketched 67 sent / 3 pending. But a lead who replied or bounced is permanently out of the sequence — they will never receive follow-up 2, no matter how long you wait. Put them in “pending” and that number never reaches zero, and the client will reasonably conclude the feature is broken.

Campaign 1 makes it concrete. 99 leads were emailed. 44 got follow-up 1, 41 are genuinely waiting on the clock — and 14 will never get it, because they already replied or bounced. Two columns cannot say that. Three can.

Finding B · needs a decision before 31 Aug

The schedule almost certainly does not mean what it looks like

Every campaign is configured 7 / 14 / 21 / 28 / 35 — the unmistakable signature of “weekly: day 7, day 14, day 21…”. But the system reads each number as a gap from the previous email, not a day on the calendar. So the real schedule is day 7, day 21, day 42, day 70, day 105.

The last follow-up lands three and a half months later than a weekly reading would put it. Nobody has noticed because follow-up 1 is identical under both readings — day 7 either way — and follow-up 2 has never fired on any campaign yet. Campaign 2 is the first, due around 31 August.

This is why the schedule view must print the absolute day and the real date, not just the configured number. A design that only echoes “14 days” back at the client keeps this invisible forever.

§4Analytics — follow-ups across every campaign

Meeting point 4. The existing Sequence step performance panel counts leads queued to Instantly, which is why it reads “Email 1 — 100/100 · 100%” and never mentions a follow-up. Queued is not sent. Replacing that measure is most of the work here.

§5Leads tab — from a number to a worklist

Not asked for, but one chip turns every count above into something the sales team can act on. A number tells you 41 leads are waiting; a filter shows you which 41.

Four decisions I need from you

  • Finding B — is the schedule wrong? If 7 / 14 / 21 / 28 / 35 was meant as weekly, the gaps need to become 7 / 7 / 7 / 7 / 7 on every campaign. Worth settling before campaign 2's follow-up 2 goes out around 31 August.
  • Do you want the third bucket? I recommend sent / due next / stopped. If the client insists on two columns, say so and I'll fold stopped into a footnote — but the pending figure will then never reach zero.
  • Where does the cross-campaign matrix live? Dashboard gives it top billing; a column on the Campaigns list keeps it next to the campaigns themselves. My preference is the Campaigns list.
  • Is “due next” enough, or do you want overdue? A separate red overdue state is possible — a lead past its due date that Instantly has not sent, usually daily-limit throttling. Useful, but it needs a tolerance window agreed so normal throttling does not read as a fault.
